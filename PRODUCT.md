# TicTic product record

A living record of the product’s purpose, signature, constraints, and release history. The detailed change list is in [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md); this page records why the product is shaped this way and what it does not claim.

## Product promise

TicTic is a quiet, local-first time companion: a clear Clock home, dependable alarms, quick timer/stopwatch, routines, time zones and intentionally designed home widgets. Core timekeeping stays useful without network access, an account, ads, remote analytics, or third-party app/runtime code. Optional weather is off by default and network use is scoped to a user-enabled widget; optional usage metrics remain local.

## Signature, not imitation

**TicTic signature:** a calm timepiece identity, ordinal uppercase date under the live clock, “Clock” centered in a slim scrollable rail, a **Usage** destination with a custom trend glyph, soft botanical color and widget designs named by their use—not a pile of recolored clones. A placed widget keeps its task-specific hierarchy while users can change its typography, content, size, spacing, silhouette and surface. Widget Studio opens in a centered popup occupying about 90% of the display; its actual RemoteViews preview stays in view as the editor scrolls. Colors are chosen from named swatches, recent colors or a visual Hue/Saturation/Brightness picker.

**TicTic widget glass:** a shaded translucent panel with a tunable matte-to-glossy highlight and fine edge. It is a static RemoteViews treatment, not a blurred screenshot or a copy of another platform. Text and widget content are rendered above the surface. Users can choose Outline only or No panel instead. Wallpaper matching uses Android WallpaperColors metadata only, only after the per-widget option is selected; unavailable metadata uses a sample palette and requests no permission.

**TicTic app glass (v1.10–1.11):** an optional user-tuned app material inspired by the supplied Liquid Glass reference, not a copied screen. Frosted, Luminous and Sculpted characters combine 15–100% opacity, matte-to-gloss sheen, adjustable depth and a visible reflection band/rim, previewed live with the selected slider values. Shared surfaces carry through Clock, Reminders, Timer, Stopwatch, Routines, Usage, full-page Settings, Widget Studio/catalog chrome and the ringing surface; widget previews remain faithful to the widget’s own chosen treatment. It uses drawn gradients/highlights, never screenshots or live blur. Critical alarm controls use a contrast-preserving opacity floor. Standard, Light, Dark and System appearance remain available. Contrast still requires visual validation on target devices.

The full-page Settings, custom alarm editor and Widget Studio use a shared accent and surface system. Sixteen app accents, broader widget ink/surface palettes and app/widget corner/spacing controls let users adapt the identity without turning the Clock screen into a theme marketplace.

## Design references and interpretation

Reference work was about capabilities and design principles, not copied screens:

- Android Authority’s clock-widget roundup describes a range from simple analog/date clocks to highly customizable designs, with size, font, color, transparency and optional motion choices. TicTic translates that breadth into a curated 20-entry widget gallery, a faithful placement preview and per-instance controls rather than importing templates or scripts: [The best Android clock widgets and weather widgets](https://www.androidauthority.com/best-android-clock-widgets-weather-clock-widgets-883303/).
- Android’s widget configuration guidance supports a configuration activity and the `reconfigurable` widget feature so users can revisit per-widget settings after placement. TicTic uses the standard AppWidget configuration flow; the launcher decides whether/how the reconfigure action appears: [Enable users to configure app widgets](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/glance/configuration), [AppWidgetProviderInfo reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/appwidget/AppWidgetProviderInfo).
- Google Clock, Alarmy and Fossify Clock remain reference points for timekeeping breadth, alarm customization and local-first operation. TicTic is not a clone and does not claim sleep tracking, Wear OS, anti-dismissal wake-up missions or universal OEM lock-screen widgets.

## Measurement, control and efficiency

- **Removed:** battery percentage snapshots. Android does not provide ordinary apps with reliable per-widget/per-screen mAh attribution, so TicTic no longer displays or infers battery drain.
- **User-controlled:** usage metrics and chronological activity history each have their own switch. Metrics include visible foreground/Clock durations, action aggregates and widget render counts; they are not energy readings. Existing local data remains until cleared.
- **Battery Saver:** when the user leaves adaptive behavior enabled and Android’s Power Saver is active, TicTic pauses seconds, reduces foreground redraw cadence and entrance animation, and asks placed TextClock views to render at minute precision. It does not alter alarm schedules or disable notifications.
- **No background seconds service:** widget time uses Android TextClock; widget updates are launcher/system and data-event driven. The app avoids persistent polling for the clock.
- **Reconfiguration and lifetime:** placed widget settings are restored into Widget Studio; saving updates that app-widget ID. Deleting a widget removes its per-instance preferences and can be included in local history.
