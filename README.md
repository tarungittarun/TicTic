# TicTic — local-first Android clock

TicTic is a native Android clock, alarm and daily-routine app. Core clock, alarms, reminders, timers, stopwatch, routines, styles and usage history stay local/offline. Weather is a separate opt-in add-on available only for selected widgets; it uses the internet only after the user enables it for that widget. TicTic has no account, ads, remote analytics or third-party app/runtime libraries.

## Current version · v1.11.2 (debug candidate; device QA pending)

- **Clock and alarms:** live time, day-part greeting, ordinal uppercase date, digital/analog faces, 12-hour AM/PM/System/24-hour formats, upcoming alarm list and pre-alarm reminders. Alarm creation prioritizes the draggable analogue time dial, AM/PM, a working Repeat switch and selected weekdays or a one-time date; secondary options are collapsed under More settings. New-alarm defaults are configurable in Settings → Alarms, with per-alarm overrides. New alarms default to a short, distinct TicTic ping 30 minutes early; each alarm’s lead time and sound are customizable, and the editor offers a bounded five-second audition for the selected alarm and reminder tones before saving. The alarm dial changes the time only when the initial touch begins inside its visible dial face, so gestures beside the dial remain available to scroll the editor. Before saving, TicTic explains exact-alarm, notification and (Android 14+) full-screen access, with consequences for declining. A reminder whose lead time has passed is called out; cards show if scheduling is inexact. Alarm create/edit now uses an opaque full-screen compact-screen editor: heading/actions stay pinned around the scrolling form, the soft keyboard resizes the form, and edit Save is emphasized. The alarm editor has explicit AM/PM selection, and routine/reminder time editing shares the same TicTic clock dial. Home Clock now offers five digital and four analog styles. The active ringing screen and notification expose only Snooze and Stop (or Stop only when selected in Settings → Alarms); Stop always remains available and stops this ring without disabling a repeating schedule. The separate pre-alarm reminder may still have its own Turn off alarm action. Alarm delivery rechecks saved enabled state to reject stale queued broadcasts. Snoozes stay visible with Cancel snooze. Volume Up and Volume Down each have independent **Snooze** or **Stop** settings for the active ringing screen. Existing shared-key choices migrate to both keys safely: legacy Control volume becomes Snooze; legacy alarm Turn off becomes Stop. Snooze uses the configured interval; Stop ends the current ring but leaves repeating schedules enabled. Settings → Alarms now separates a dedicated “While an alarm is ringing” card from alarm sound controls: choose Snooze + Stop or Stop only for the full-screen ring and notification, then assign Volume Up and Volume Down independently. The controls explain that key shortcuts apply only while the full-screen alarm is active and do not alter visible buttons. Stop cannot be hidden and only stops this ring. There is no Turn off action on the active ring surface. When the display is off, wake the device and use lock-screen notification actions when available. Android reserves the Power key, so TicTic cannot remap it. Time zones include EST/EDT, IST and GMT. The alarm editor persists edits by stable ID, reschedules the changed alarm and exposes custom reminder minutes immediately. v1.9.6 fixes the previously blank create/edit dialog by attaching the custom editor shell to its dialog window; its on-device visibility still needs target-phone verification. Timer accepts hours, minutes and seconds with a live, theme-aware picker preview; its countdown uses a monotonic deadline, pauses/resumes from the exact remaining duration, re-anchors across reboot/time changes, and posts a completion notification. Settings can enable a hundredths display, button haptics, completion alerts and completion vibration; Android 12+ users are told when completion alerts may be delayed without Alarms & reminders access. Stopwatch elapsed time is monotonic, hundredths can be shown or hidden without reducing stored lap precision, and button/lap haptics are independently configurable. Routine and Usage data remain local.
- **Reminders:** placed directly after Clock in navigation. Reminder and routine names are optional; blank names save as the next unused `Reminder 01`, `Reminder 02`… or `Routine 01`, `Routine 02`… label. Reminder Add/Edit “More settings” includes TicTic Ping, Daybreak Bell, Golden Hour, Pulse, system notification/custom phone tones, silent mode, vibration, and a per-reminder ring duration (1–30 seconds). Settings supplies default tone, duration, vibration and snooze length. A bounded media-playback service stops after the chosen duration and reminder cancellation/dismissal requests stop playback; Android notification permission, channel volume, Do Not Disturb and OEM behavior still affect audibility. Master Settings also offers Routine schedule defaults/notifications, Timer default/alert/vibration controls, and Stopwatch precision/lap haptics. Empty and populated lists each show exactly one Add reminder button. Reminder cards open their editor on tap; there is no separate Edit button. Dismiss completes a one-time reminder or skips the next occurrence of a weekly reminder; notification Snooze follows the configurable 5/10/15/30-minute setting. Default vibration is configurable. Schedules are restored after reboot and time changes; notification permission is requested contextually, while Android may deliver reminders inexactly without exact-alarm access.
- **Refined app UI:** the Activity/Log destination is labeled **Usage**, with a custom trend-chart glyph; sixteen accent colors, app-wide font, corner, border, elevation and spacing choices, system/light/dark appearance, and stronger secondary-text contrast. Settings is a full-page screen with a category rail and independently bounded scrolling. Compact screens browse categories horizontally; wider screens retain a left rail. Option changes preserve scroll position; switching groups starts at that group’s top. Card elevation changes shadow and surface contrast. Design now offers five ready-made bubble-glass moods—**Sunlit Bubble, Daylight Pearl, Golden Hour, Moonlit Opal and Aurora Frost**—with selection, live preview, and editable surface opacity, sheen, depth and panel styling. Theme-aware highlights and an 82% opacity floor keep glass behind text/content; critical actions remain solid. Buttons/cards expose five selectable press styles—**Water ripple, Gel squeeze, Expressive spring, Glass wave and Magnetic lift**—with intensity, preview, Reduce motion, and Android system-animation checks. The renderer is a drawn layered highlight, not live screen blur or a screenshot. See [the interaction study](Gel-Interaction-Preview.html) for a prototype, not an Android runtime capture.
- **20 curated, resize-aware provider entries:** five core clock designs plus fifteen selections from existing providers. All 24 registered widget providers advertise horizontal/vertical resizing plus 150×84 dp minimum-resize hints on Android 12+; the widget renderer scales clock typography and hides secondary content at compact sizes. Launchers/OEMs retain final control over whether these hints are honored and how small a widget can become. The existing 20-item curation is retained; the separate request for 15 genuinely new widget concepts remains outstanding. Existing placements stay supported. See [`widget_specs.txt`](widget_specs.txt) and the [responsive screen/design board](TICTIC-Design-Board.html).
- **Widget Studio:** compact or short-height screens use a full-screen editor; larger/taller screens use a centered bounded popup. The header/live RemoteViews preview and Cancel/Apply footer stay pinned around a separately scrolling control form. The preview applies the actual widget layout and style; controls are grouped by widget capability and include a contrast estimate. Glass presets can be selected and then customized, with the chosen preset’s glow/base retained in preview and saved widget rendering. Wallpaper matching is opt-in and uses Android wallpaper color metadata only—no wallpaper image, picker or permission prompt; a sample palette is used when colors are unavailable. Weather is shown only on supported widgets and only when individually enabled; city entry is manual and update interval is selected per widget.
- **Re-edit placed widgets:** on Android 9+ the provider metadata advertises reconfiguration. Launchers that honor it may offer Configure from the widget long-press menu; launcher/OEM support varies. Widget Studio reloads that widget’s saved settings and applies changes in place.
- **Widget-use history:** the local Usage screen records widget add/style changes, opens and removals when activity history is enabled; chronological history stays available within Usage.
- **User-controlled metrics:** separate Settings switches control local usage counters and activity history. Usage offers a visually segmented Today / 7-day / 30-day time window with a date-range caption; summary totals and Recent history follow the selected window. The Recent history section starts with 20 entries and can expand to 100. Users can save an optional CSV usage report using Android’s document picker; it includes period totals and retained local activity rows (which may include alarm/routine labels). Export is not automatic and does not upload data. These are counters/estimates, not battery attribution.
- **Battery Saver:** when enabled by the user, TicTic adapts while Android Battery Saver is active: second-level clock updates pause, foreground redraws slow, entrance animation is skipped, and widget TextClock precision drops to minutes. A separate setting controls seconds, and metrics/history can each be disabled.

## Widget treatments and signature

### Optional widget weather

Weather is off by default and available only for supported clock widgets. Turning it on for one widget enables network use for that widget only; city is entered manually, so no location permission is requested. Choose 30, 60 or 120 minutes. Android periodic work is inexact and may be deferred; displayed intervals are requests, not guaranteed update times. Internet connectivity is a normal install-time Android permission, not a runtime prompt. When disabled, no weather requests are made and all non-weather features remain offline. The selected free Open-Meteo endpoint is **non-commercial only** and its weather data uses CC BY 4.0 attribution. Do not ship an ad-supported, subscription or otherwise commercial integration on the free endpoint; obtain a paid plan first.

TicTic glass uses a tinted translucent panel with a matte-to-glossy static highlight. Widget text stays above the panel; it is not a blurred screenshot or an iOS screen copy. Wallpaper-reactive colors use only Android `WallpaperColors` metadata when the user enables that option. API <27, null colors and read failures fall back to the sample palette without requesting a permission or opening a picker.

## Battery metrics removed

TicTic no longer samples or displays battery percentage snapshots. The app does not attempt to estimate per-widget or per-screen battery drain. On first launch after upgrading, unused v1.3 battery snapshot preferences are removed.

## Usage and history controls

Counters and activity history are stored on-device. **Record usage metrics** can be turned off to stop foreground-duration, widget-refresh and action counters; **Keep activity history** can be turned off to stop appending chronological history. Existing entries remain until the user clears them or clears app data. Clearing history is available on the Usage screen. Widget-render counts are not usage time or energy measurements.

## Lock-screen compatibility

Android does not provide one universal API for ordinary third-party home-screen widgets to appear on modern lock screens. TicTic does **not** promise that its widgets can be pinned to every lock screen. It provides Android's next-alarm information, alarm notifications and an alarm ringing surface where OS/user settings permit. OEM surfaces, notification privacy and full-screen-intent policies differ; verify on the target phone.

## Compatibility, privacy and resources

- `minSdk 23` (Android 6.0), `targetSdk 34`. Exact alarm delivery, launcher reconfiguration, widgets and lock-screen surfaces vary by Android release and OEM.
- The manifest includes `INTERNET` for the optional weather feature, but it has no runtime location permission and performs no weather network request until the user enables weather for an eligible widget. The manually entered city is sent to Open-Meteo geocoding/forecast endpoints; cached result and widget settings stay in app-private local storage. Every other core feature remains offline. Android may defer periodic jobs; the chosen 30/60/120-minute cadence is best-effort, not guaranteed.
- No third-party app/runtime dependencies; local vector/XML art and three bundled alarm tones are used. Weather uses Open-Meteo’s public API only after per-widget opt-in. Its free endpoint is non-commercial; any commercial product, ad-supported or subscription distribution must switch to a suitable paid plan. Open-Meteo data attribution and CC BY 4.0 are disclosed in Studio and `widget_specs.txt`. Widget updates are event-driven/launcher-managed, with TextClock doing the time display; there is no high-frequency background clock service.
- Android 12+ uses optional special **Alarms & reminders** access for precise scheduling; TicTic explains and requests it before saving alarms. Without it, alarms, early pings and local reminders use an inexact fallback and may be late. Android 13+ notification access is optional to save but is needed for early-ping and actionable alarm notifications. On Android 14+, full-screen alarm access is separate and optional; without it, Android may not show the large ringing view over the lock screen. Notification channels and OEM/battery policy can also affect delivery.

## Build

Install JDK 11 or later and Android SDK Platform 34. From the project root:

```sh
bash gradlew assembleDebug lint
```

The debug APK is produced at `app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`; it is for local installation/testing, not production store distribution. The current [v1.11.2 debug APK](artifacts/TicTic-v1.11.2-debug.apk), SHA-256 and signature record are in [`ARTIFACTS-v1.11.2.md`](ARTIFACTS-v1.11.2.md); build/test results and runtime-QA limitations are in [`TESTING-v1.11.2.md`](TESTING-v1.11.2.md).
